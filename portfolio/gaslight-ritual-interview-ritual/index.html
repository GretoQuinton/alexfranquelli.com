‘Gaslight’ is a surprising album

Ritual magazine – Aghast Manor - Gaslights ‘Gaslight’ is a surprising album. It is made up of different layers you discover only by listening to it time after time. How do you write your music? What is the process you follow to compose tracks with such a simple but effective structure?

- Well, all my ideas firstly start in my mind... the music, visions, concepts... they are made in my head, and some songs I make in my dreams even. Such as the song ‘La Petite Mort’, which was the last song I made for the ‘Gaslight’ album. I dreamt that I was in a dark, atmospheric city (I love South-European cities by the way! I love Italy!) and I suddenly was on a theatre stage doing a bizarre play. One of the actors was Andy LaPlegua from Combichrist actually, hehe... and the stage-play finished with me singing this strange song. When I woke up, I just knew I had to have it on ‘Gaslights’... weird but kinda cool... I see my songs as ‘pieces of art’, as expressions that represent something psychological, thus I pay greater attention to the atmosphere than to technical music playing... Particularly with ambient soundscapes like the songs on Aghast Manor I think it is really important to create real atmosphere and let it speak to the listener to create visions in their minds. When I’m in the studio, I try to be in the right state of mind in order to make sounds and melodies that speak to me, that give me the right kind of feeling. Creating sinister creepy soundtracks appears to be quite a talent of mine, obviously I am no stranger to that kind of dark and fearful thoughts and emotions, they have been with me since I am a child, thus it is easy for me to bring these feelings across to others. Just out of curiosity: is there any contemporary composer you admire? I can hear a lot of Edgard Varèse’s ‘Poeme Electronique’ for some unknown reason. What is your relationship with avant-garde? - I am a big fan of 1920/30’s cinema, silent movies like the early Nosferatu and also the amazing Nosferatu version with Klaus Kinski, which has the most amazing opening scenes and opening music theme ever... so with songs like ‘Dance The Hanged Man’s Jig’ I had such films in my mind. Otherwise I really love the soundtracks of both ‘The Fountain’ and ‘Requiem for a Dream’. But I don’t think any composer in particular comes to my mind that inspired my music, no. Tell us something more about this thing with Megan Fox. What happened with her? Did she really hear ‘Das Irrlicht’ and got scared? - Haha, yes that is a funny story... and it basically was the reason I started to think about making ambient music again. This happened about three years ago I think, as I got more involved with film-scripts, film-makers and actors. I had just signed up for Twitter where I ended up on this celebraty page and thus got involved with Perez Hilton of all people who was ‘slagging off’ Megan Fox for being a ‘dopehead’ because she smokes weed... It was Halloween when Megan Fox suddenly posted a weird Twitter message saying “uh I’m so bored, I need something to freak me out, does anybody know some real spooky music”? So I suddenly thought of my very old project Aghast and thus I sent her a youtube link of ‘Das Irrlicht’ and wrote “hey, turn off your lights and smoke a joint to THAT one” ... I sent the link to Perez Hilton as well and both were pretty much disturbed by the ‘sick’ song because this was “some REAL shit...” and not some Hollywood horror, hehe. And with it I remembered that I was pretty good at making creepy music and got inspired to do a new project like that. My sinister soundtracks seem to be quite popular amongst film-makers... also in Hollywood, which is nice of course... What fascinates me with Aghast Manor is the fact that the border between music and imagery is subtle or – sometimes – non-existing. Is there a different approach when you have to compose soundtracks? - Yes, with Aghast Manor the themes, visions and music come together as one, thus creating a song. With other soundtracks and my ‘Spoken Words’ album with my poems from “The Dark Side Of Dreaming” book, I have to build the sounds and melodies around a theme, an image or a poem that is already there. Thus I underline the atmosphere of the image or the poem. Being an artist, I can only work with themes that inspire me, of course. For example, I was asked by a female RnB singer if I could outline scenes and story ideas for her video, and than she sent me the song, and seriously, I found that song so boring that I saw nothing at all in my head. So I told her “I’m sorry, I don’t see any images to your song at all, you should ask someone else...“

Could you tell us something on the new Scott Derrickson’s movie? How did this collaboration come about? - Scott sent me an e-mail and told me that he liked my music and that it would fit to his new film called Sinister. He made major Hollywood movies like ‘The Day The Earth Stood Still’ with actor Keanu Reves and also ‘The Exorcism Of Emily Rose’... so I gave him a few songs and we e-mailed back and forth and we are still in touch... he is a really nice guy and supports real, dark and deep artists like myself... I’ve also been corresponding with the team of David Lynch and I hope that something will happen there, but as of yet, I don’t know. I see that you’re interested in the concept of ‘shame’. Blaise Pascal used to say that ‘The only shame is to have none’. What are yours? Is there any room for shame left in such an exhibitionist society like ours? - Yes my works, especially my personal works are very much based on dark psychological aspects, hidden emotions, fears and thus shame. I’m not really talking about ‘general shame’ like nudity or something. I talk more about the thoughts, feelings and situations that we hide from the world because we could never tell of them. The secrets we keep, family secrets, forbidden affairs, bad things we have done. I’ve always been fascinated by human nature and the things we keep hidden. I often say that I write about what I know, which indeed I do. I grew up with quite bad childhood experiences, fears and indeed things I could not tell to anyone. I also grew up with secrets the family tried to hide away. This is the kind of shame I talk about, the personal experiences and thoughts we better keep to ourselves... By looking at your numerous projects (books, several music projects, fashion, a label…), you seem to have a bit of an entrepreneurial spirit. Is there something you would like to do but you haven’t had a chance to focus on yet? - Oh there are so many things I have not done yet. I love to explore new ideas and I seek new challenges all the time. I’m quite restless and I get bored easily. I do music, write novels, short stories and philosophical books and I try to get more into films and acting. I also have entrepreneur ideas and create designs with cheeky statements for postcards and t-shirts. I still like to get a perfume line going as well. On top of that, I would love to get better at kickboxing and martial arts. I have always been into fighting sports and one of my dreams has been to compete in MMA fights but hmm, I guess I just keep that as a hobby as it takes very hard work to become good and I would not have anymore time for my artistic activities. And than I still have many dreams to fulfill and many things I like to do, many countries to visit, but time is limited...

Again on your several (music) outlets. Is there a scene you feel particularly attached to? - I like to consider myself to be individual both when it comes to music-making and scenes. I have been part of many scenes and I found all of them to be rather restrictive to my personal- and artistic freedom. I enjoy working with all kinds of artistic people, be they from known commercial crowds or from subculture scenes. But of course I consider myself coming from the gothic music scene; the early new wave, synth-pop, electro, punkrock and industrial music scene. That has been my influence and I still love the whole scene. I also rose from the esoteric art scene, you know, I used to hang with people like Genesis P. Orrige, Boyd Rice, friends of William Burroughs, the people from infamous Hammer movies and Redemption movies etc... So even though I am open to work with everybody interesting, it is the gothic and dark art scenes I feel mostly connected to...

Dark ambient, folk, non-music: I am reasonably sure you do not describe your music using with none of these labels. How would you define it then? - Yes indeed, I don’t like to put any limiting labels to myself or my music, I just like to create everything from ambient to electronic to rock to accoustic to whatever, and I guess I describe my music a little different than others. Aghast Manor I would call a blend of avant garde, ambient, neo-classical and industrial. For the next Aghast Manor album I want to use more electronic sounds actually. My other, or more personal songs, as Andréa Nebel and Nebelhexe, I would simply call alternative rock. Or I guess ‘gothic’ defines the music as a whole. I just like to explore new ideas and thus keep the genre open. My music and different music-styles are often misunderstood unfortunately and thus put into boxes they do not belong... but hey, with it they have been put into all types of genres imaginable... so my music surely circulates everywhere, hehe.
