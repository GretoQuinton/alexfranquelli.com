Ulver - interview

Archive source

Full text recovered from Alex Franquelli’s author archive.

All: Even recently, people have asked me exactly what Ulver is and the best answer I’ve been able to give is that Ulver is something that keeps on changing, therefore Ulver does not exist per se. It exists on its albums, then and there, but nowhere else. Now that you’ve added the live dimension, I have to think about something else (possibly equally smart) to say. So I ask you: what is Ulver ?

Krystoffer: I have to agree with you when you say that silence is the music of the future and in doing so, I am in good company, since a composer such as John Cage was of the same opinion; but while his silence was conceived as “music” in itself, I reckon that in our time, it is indeed a necessity and a means to escape the routine and the opulence (even in times of economic crisis) of our society. Is it what you mean when you refer to silence as the sound of the future ?

Krystoffer: I heard you defined “Shadows Of The Sun” as the most obscure albums ever composed by Ulver and I tend to agree with that. I’ve always thought that you guys have taken out the word “metal” from the label “black metal” and that is an achievement in itself. But what’s left of the old Ulver in your more recent music ?

Krystoffer and Tore: I’ve always thought that there’s always a lot of improvisation in your albums, is it the case this time as well ?

Krystoffer and Tore: You’ve almost completely stripped your music of guitars, of conventional drumming, of a recognizable song structure, of anything definable as “rock” and god knows what else. A question everybody must have asked but for which I hope you don’t have an answer: where is your music heading to ? Is this contemporary chamber music ?

All: your last records have developed a certain taste for minimalism which is rather evident in repeating mantras, a slow climax which leads nowhere but at the centre of the song. How did your passion for contemporary music came about ?

All: I can hear some Philip Glass in “Blood Inside” and “Shadows Of The Sun” and this can only increase thanks to Daniel’s contribution. Is it the case on this new record as well ?

Daniel: How did this collaboration begin ? I’ve read it was all thanks to myspace. Is it true ?

Daniel: I love how you bring contemporary music in all of your projects. I can hear some Messiaen in Aethenor, Steve Reich in Miasma, Magma (which I know Krys likes as well) in Guapo and I know about your passion for Pan Sonic and probably Lamonte Young (from what I can hear), krautrock with Mothlite and Chrome Hoof, Dumitrescu with Sunn O))) and then progressive music in general. How much of this will be on the new album?

Daniel: I know that you’re currently recording an album with Mothlite. How are things going ?

All: is there a visual concept behind this new album ? Will images still play an important part live and will they still be prepared by your friends Jørn and Kristin Bøyesen?

All: I reckon the previous albums were not written keeping in mind that the songs were going to be played live. This is probably one of (if not “the”) first time this is happening. How is this shaping up the way you are writing and recording ?

All: have you learnt to cope with fear now ? If so, how ?
