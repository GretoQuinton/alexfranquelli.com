Karate (monograph)

Alex Franquelli

Questions for Geoff Farina

Can you tell me how you met Gavin and Eamonn at University in 1993? Did you have a clear idea of what music you wanted to play?

I’ve read that some of your influences include contemporary music (Philip Glass, Steve Reich...). What part did this interest have in the making of the first albums and of the band altogether ?

As young boys, you guys must have been aware of the direction music was taking at the beginning of the 90’s. Grunge was already moving waves in the US and I am sure you, as a band, must have felt that indie music was changing from the inside. How come, then, your sound was so un-grunge? My impression is that you guys were more punk than most grunge bands of the time, but you were somehow out of that scene because although you shared a common ground with most bands, you remained loyal to a certain aesthetic and took it to a different level. “Death Kit” (1993) and “Karate” (1996) show a mature sound, free from the raw naivety of most contemporary bands.

What happened between “In Place Of Real Insight" and “The Bed Is In The Ocean”? Karate sound like a totally different band. Did playing live play a role in your evolution?

Is it true that some lyrics (if so, which ones?) have been written using the automatic writing method ?

Where do you think Karate would be today without the unfortunate problem you have developed? Do you think their maturation process was completed with “Pockets” ?

How did you meet Jodi Buonanno?

Do you have any regret with regards to this collaboration? I mean: there was a lot of potential (i.e. the Narragansett Grange Hall) which was not related to the music and which we, in Europe, did not manage to see.

As you can easily imagine, in my monograph I should also focus on your relationship with Italy and the Italian audience. Could you explain what it has meant to you and how you developed it ?

Have you guys ever been upset with the amount of criticism you received from the rock press when your music started to include jazz and fusion elements? Some of them accused you of being “snob” or, worse, self-congratulatory.
