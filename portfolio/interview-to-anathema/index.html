Interview to Anathema

Archive source

Full text recovered from Alex Franquelli’s author archive.

How do I take “Dreaming Light” off my head? I had the same problem with “Inner Silence” !

Before we start: considering that “A Natural Disaster” has been described by yourself as a “solo” album, why such a long time has passed since “A Fine Day To Exit” came out? I mean, in 9 years many bands go from their parents’ garage to filling up stadiums, back to their jobs in supermarkets. What happened in the Anathema camp?

Your intentions with regards to the new album was to try and keep it as intimate an affair as possible for many reasons and up until a couple of years ago you didn’t even know whether or not someone would have produced it. Then Steve Wilson came in. How did things evolve and how was it working with him?

Your case is quite emblematic; a great band which, for many reasons, decides to retain full control of its music. Is this the natural return to origins, has the bubble burst or is it just the way things go when you refuse to go mainstream?

Many people consider Anathema as being a progressive rock band and this, in my opinion, is quite wrong. Your music today is incidentally close to progressive but its structure, sound, feeling are not progressive at all. In my review of the album I stressed the”dynamic” component rather than the progressive one; in fact there is a lot of work on the dynamics rather than on pure progression. Does the fact that people try to label you simply as “progressive”?

You’ve gone through a very tough period with so many problems which would have destroyed 98% of the bands around. What will make you feel it’s all been worth the struggle/effort?

Can you tell me something about “A Natural Disaster” ? What does that album mean specifically to you and did it close a chapter in your life?

In a recent interview I’ve heard you say that you were not entirely satisfied with your previous albums - which as someone who really appreciates your music, I found impossible to believe. Is it true? If so, what is the reason behind this feeling?

So what makes the new album “better” and more complete?

The impression I have now, after almost 20 years as your listener, is that you simply “passed through” the metal phase as it actually wasn’t the reason why you put the band together in the first place.

How do I explain this ! Your new album is slightly darker than the previous ones but...my personal opinion is that it is also a tad less nostalgic, melancholic than the previous ones. There is a sense of “light at the end of the tunnel”. You’ve always liked the relationship with your audience when playing live and this was in contrast with the “dark” feeling of your previous album. Is this part of a maturation process? Many of the songs on the new album are built on crescendos, climaxes (thumping drumming, build-ups, a theme that is developed throughout the song, etc...) as if they came out of jams in the studio. Has your approach to composition changed with the new album?

Did you follow a different path to write the album or have the songs developed spontaneously? I’ve always thought that most of your songs had acoustic origins; in other words were born from playing acoustic, while in “We are here...” I can feel the electric dimension. Is that the case?

I reckon your music has always been very “visual” but with “We’re here...” it has reached a point where, in my opinion, it is ready for being the soundtrack of a movie or documentary. Have you ever considered the idea or have you ever been offered the chance to write for the cinema?

Are you still in a good relationship with Duncan Patterson? Do you appreciate his new material (Ion, Antimatter) ?

Last night you didn’t seem too fussed by the fact that you knew that many people had downloaded your new album. What is your take on peer-to-peer technology?

Completely out of curiosity: how do you guys distinguish a band argument from a family argument?

What are you listening to these days?
