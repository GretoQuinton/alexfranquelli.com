Interview with Deadfly Ensemble By Alex Franquelli For Ritual

When James and Lucas started back in 2005, the direction the duo was taking was slightly more acoustic and I think that although we couldn’t define the band as ‘folk’, there certainly were some connections with popular music. With time the band has grown and its music has changed with it, but the similarities with the original setting of the band are still there. I find your inspiration to be deeply rooted in European music: how can an American band capture that ‘euro-feeling’ without sounding farcical? I reckon you sound incredibly more European than a band of Germans playing in Brussels.

While lots of people seem to put you into the art-rock niche, I think that a comparison with more ‘folksy’ ensembles like Gogol Bordello or Beirut is not completely out of order. Do you get your inspiration from old, popular music as well? I think that your choice of instruments somehow betrays a penchant for traditional music.

Could you tell us something about the lyrics? What is your source of inspiration?

The new album seems more song-oriented than the previous releases. How does the composition process work? I always seem to discover new layers in the songs.

The new album is somehow closer to Cinema Strange (especially with regards to the guitar sound) than the previous releases as Deadfly Ensemble. What is there in store for C.S.?

What is the reason for choosing to come out with a record, which is lo-fi in a time when the digitalisation of sound has made all albums equally, polished and consequently flat?

The elaborated artwork of the CD is somehow opposed to the aforementioned lo-fi dimension to the album. I know Lucas is responsible for it: is there a concept behind it?

I know your live shows are always something special: any plan of coming to Europe? If so, what do we have to expect?

Thank you for your time !
