Interview with Horseback - Ritual

Archive source

Full text recovered from Alex Franquelli’s author archive.

On one of my reviews for “The Invisible Mountain” I wrote: “Many bands can play. Only a few of those can play with drones. Almost none of those can take drones and repetitions to the next level, and just one sounds as if Tony Conrad and Terry Riley were toying with cellos and vibraphones behind the scenes while a rock band is playing on stage”. And that band is obviously Horseback. How important is contemporary music for your songwriting and is it an inspiration?

“Half Blood” appears to be more organic, with a focus on layers of sounds rather than the sonic flow of tracks such as, for example, “Tyrant Symmetry” on “The Invisible Mountain”. And this, in my opinion, makes the new album more accessible and more experimental at the same time. When you write music, do you have a vague idea of the ideal boundary between accessibility and experimentation? If not, is there a boundary at all?

Another thing I have always wanted to ask you is: could yours be a new type of folk? Psychedelic music started from folk (Kaleidoscope, Peanut Butter Conspiracy, Grateful Dead…), went somewhere else with Soft Machine, Jimi Hendrix ,stoner and even electronica. With Horseback and a bunch of other bands, it appears that folk is making its way back into the music. What do you think?

As I’ve mentioned, I feel that the songwriting on the new album is relatively different from your previous full-length and the more recent releases. What has changed in the way you write and what can you say are your strongest influences right now?

A seemingly dull question now, but one which one feels compelled to ask with bands such as yours: totally unpredictable and inspired. Where do you think your music will take you? The evolution of Horseback is almost tangible and if you ask me how I see Coldplay will evolve in the next few years, well, I am confident I know the answer. But what about Horseback? The connection between rock, avant-garde and folk or psychedelic rock is one that could take you anywhere.

Still with regards to this, do you feel there is a scene in the US where bands with influences such as yours are starting to emerge?

Your metal roots are somehow pretty much part of what Horseback are nowadays. How attached are you still to the metal scene and what are you listening to right now (metal or not)?

Jenks, I have to say I still haven’t had a chance to listen to any of your other projects. Are you collaborating with someone at the moment or do you have any plan of starting a collaboration?

A question on the beautiful video for “Blood Fountain”. I know that a friend of yours, Jon Mackey, is behind that little minimalist jewel. Will we see something similar for one of the tracks of the new album?

On another note, I don’t say anything new if I say that your music is the perfect soundtrack for a movie which does not exist. I know that you’ve been inspired by Tarkovsky’s sci-fi approach to cinema for “Forbidden Planet”. Has anything similar happened for “Half Blood”?

Will we have a chance to see you guys live in Europe any time soon?

Thank you very much for your time! I do hope to see you guys on a stage very soon !
