Interview with Cold Body Radiation for Ritual

Questions by Alex Franquelli

Heavy pop or post-black metal shoegaze? How unimportant are labels for a band, which blissfully merges more than one genre in a way that makes them also perfectly unrecognisable?

Do you think that without your black metal background, you would have a bigger audience? Many people may let your biography get in the way of your music. For as stupid as it sounds, it is actually quite true; after all, the other way around is very much a reality. A Burzum or a Mayhem fan would never take Explosions in the Sky or Mogwai seriously if these two bands changed direction and started to play black metal.

If one takes into account your two albums, it appears that your sound is still work in progress as it keeps developing and I reckon it will keep on changing record after record. Do you have an idea where your inspiration will take you next and what do you think has taken you and your music where you are right now?

I know that the previous album (“The Great White Emptiness”) started being distributed by yourself at first and only after it reached a certain level of general approval, you were offered a contract with Dusktone. Although your career as the mind behind Cold Body Radiation is relatively short, you have had a chance to experience both sides of the coin: DIY and organised distribution. What differences do you see between these 2 ways to make your music known?

Tell us something more about yourself, if you don’t mind. Where and how did you gather the experience needed to come out with albums as elaborated as the ones you already have under the belt?

What is your approach to composition? Is there any routine you (have to) follow or do ideas naturally translate themselves into music without any sort of filter?

One thing I’ve always wondered is: does being a one-man band make you feel touch with your music? Being fully immersed in it and not having any feedback coming from other bandmates is a risky business. How do you deal with it?

What is your relationship with the live dimension? Will we see you on a stage any time soon?

Thank you very much for your time! Is there something you would not like to be asked anymore apart from the usual questions on how much black metal is there on your music?
